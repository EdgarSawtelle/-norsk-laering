## 3. Grammatikk (rules), fortsettelse

### G48. Komparativ og superlativ – øvelser: to ting, *mer* + adjektiv, spesielle former *(Adj s.49 · s.51 · 2026-11-24)*
This adds to G40 (the full rule). What the worksheet shows:

**1. -ig and -som get only -st in the superlativ** (box on p.49): billig – billigere – billig**st** · hyggelig – hyggeligere – hyggelig**st** · morsom – morsommere – morsom**st**. A double consonant stays double in the comparative/superlative: sunn – sunn**ere** – sunn**est** (but the neuter loses one: *sunt*). *dyr* just adds the ending: dyr – dyrere – dyrest. Your answers *billigst · dyrest · billigst* are correct.

**2. Two things or more than two?** (ex. 29)
- With **enn** → komparativ: *Lise er høyere enn Anne.* · *Nils er eldre enn Per.*
- Without *enn*, the superlativ names "the highest degree". The book says it is used «enten vi sammenlikner to eller flere ting»: *Lise er høyest.*
- Questions with two options: the strict school rule is the **komparativ** (*Hvilken by er større, Bergen eller Trondheim?* · *Hva er bedre, å jobbe eller å gå på skolen?*). In speech *Hva er best, A eller B?* is very common and accepted, so both ticked boxes can be defended; *bedre / større* is the safe answer in a test.
- Three or more things → always **superlativ**: *Hvilke epler er billigst?*

**3. -ere/-est or mer/mest?** (ex. 31)
- Short adjectives take endings: stor**e**, fin**ere**, billig**ere**, hvit**ere**, lengre.
- Long or foreign adjectives, and those in **-ende, -isk, -ful**, use *mer / mest*: *mer populær, mer spennende, mer smakfullt*.
- **Trap:** after *mer* the adjective still **agrees** with the subject (G1): *Fotball (en) er mer populær enn ski.* · *Kjøtt (et) er mer smakfull**t** enn fisk.* · *Bananene er mer populær**e** enn druene.* The -ere form never changes: *billigere* works for en/ei/et words and plural.

**4. Special forms** (table on p.51; learn by heart):
| Positiv | Komparativ | Superlativ |
|---|---|---|
| liten | mindre | minst |
| gammel | eldre | eldst |
| lang | lengre | lengst |
| ille | verre | verst |
| stor | større | størst |
| ung | yngre | yngst |
| god | bedre | best |
| tung | tyngre | tyngst |
- **ille** (bad, badly) is the base of *verre / verst* (G40: *dårlig* also uses them). Today it mostly survives in set phrases: *ille ute* (in trouble), *fare ille* (to fare badly), *ta noe ille opp* (to take something badly).

**5. lengre vs lenger** (your answer in ex. 31): *Tokyo er **lenger** borte enn Ankara.* You wrote *lengre*. **lengre** = physically longer (*en lengre tur, et lengre skjørt*); **lenger** = further / longer as an adverb, in distance or time (*lenger borte, Jeg orker ikke lenger*). And *borte* is the place word, see G49.

### G49. Retning eller sted? bort/borte, hjem/hjemme, ut/ute … *(Adj s.51 · K7 s.105 · N p51 · 2026-11-24)*
Your note: **bort / borte → statisk**. Many Norwegian adverbs come in pairs. The form **without -e** is **direction** (movement towards, *hvor … til?*). The form **with -e** is **place** (where something is, static, *hvor?*).
| Retning (movement) | Sted (static) |
|---|---|
| bort | borte |
| hjem | hjemme |
| ut | ute |
| inn | inne |
| opp | oppe |
| ned | nede |
| fram / frem | framme / fremme |
| hit / dit | her / der |
- Movement verbs (gå, komme, dra, kjøre, løpe, flytte) → *bort, hjem, ut*: *Amalie kommer **hjem** fra skolen.* · *Barnevernet dro **hjem** til folk.* · *Jeg går **bort** til henne.*
- State verbs (være, bo, bli, sitte, stå, ligge, vente) → *borte, hjemme, ute*: *…slik at barnet kan bo **hjemme**.* · *Jeg blir **hjemme**.* · *Tokyo er lenger **borte** enn Ankara.*

**Exceptions and traps:**
- **borte** also means *gone, missing*, with no real place involved: *Nøklene er borte.*
- **bort** is the form inside particle verbs and fixed phrases of removal: *legge bort mobilen* (K6 s.84), *ta bort, kaste bort* (→ *bortkastet* = wasted), *bort fra / bort til*.
- Comparative: *lenger **bort*** (further, in a direction) vs *lenger **borte*** (further away, a place).
- *hjemmefra* (= from home) is a third form and has no pair: *Hun vil flytte hjemmefra.*
- English "home" is one word for both; in Norwegian *Jeg er hjem* is wrong: *Jeg er **hjemme**.* / *Jeg går **hjem**.*

### G50. Gjenta setningene: den/det/de + adjektiv + bestemt substantiv *(Adj s.49 · 2026-11-24)*
Ex. 24: put an adjective that fits in front of the noun, in the **definite** form (repeats G2). The article follows the **gender of the noun**, not the adjective.
| Setning | Your answer → correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jeg kjøpte boka. | *den mye boke* → **den nye boka** | *mye* (much) ≠ *nye* (new, from *ny*). *bok* is feminine (ei bok) → *boka* or *boken*, never ~~boke~~ |
| Se på katten! | **den nye katten** | en katt → *den* |
| Hvor mye koster eplene? | **de gamle eplene** | plural → *de*; gammel drops the e: gamle (G1) |
| Kan vi kjøpe blomstene? | **de gule blomstene** | plural → *de*; gul + e |
| Besøkte dere slottet? | **det fine slottet** | et slott → *det* |
- Pattern: **den** (en/ei words) · **det** (et words) · **de** (plural) + adjective + **-e** + noun with its definite ending (*-en / -a / -et / -ene*).
- *ny* is irregular in the neuter only: ny – nytt – nye. Definite: *det nye huset*, *den nye boka*, *de nye bøkene*.
- Pronunciation trap: *mye* and *nye* sound almost the same except for the first sound, so check the meaning when you write.

### G51. Sammensatte ord: fuge-e og fuge-s (barne-, besøks-, taushets-) *(N p51 · K7 s.105 · 2026-11-24)*
Your note: barn**e**hage, barn**e**vogn, barn**e**rom, barn**e**vern (the e circled). When two nouns are glued together, a **linking letter** often sits at the join (*fuge*). It isn't fully predictable, but there are patterns:
- **barn → barne-** in most compounds: barnehage, barnevogn, barnerom, barnevern, barneskole, barnebok, barnebarn (grandchild). Exceptions without e: *barndom* (childhood), *barnløs* (childless), *barnslig* (childish).
- **-s-** is usual when the first word ends in **-het, -ing, -sjon, -skap, -dom, -else**: taushet**s**plikt, utdanning**s**vei, informasjon**s**kilde, frihet**s**kamp. Also common after many other words: besøk**s**hjem, fritid**s**klubb, arbeid**s**giver.
- **No link** is also very common: fosterhjem, rusproblem, brennevin, enkeltjobb, dataspill.
- The **gender** of a compound comes from the **last** part: et barnevern (et vern), et barnerom (et rom), en barnehage (en hage), ei/en barnevogn (ei/en vogn).
- When in doubt, learn the compound as one unit and check NAOB. A wrong link (~~barnhage~~) looks odd but is still understood.
